# Why WordLens exists

## The problem

On a phone, laptop, iPad or e-reader, finding a word's meaning is one tap:
press the word and the meaning pops up, and you keep reading. Reading a
**physical book** has no such thing. You stop, pick up your phone, unlock it,
open a dictionary or Google, type the word, read the answer, and find your
place again.

That takes 5–10 seconds, and in those seconds the reading spell often breaks,
especially in a tense or gripping part of a book. The owner hit this
constantly, and it's the whole reason for this app.

## The idea

Keep the book in your hands and let the phone do the looking. Open WordLens,
hold the phone over the page, and rest the lens on the word:

- **No tapping, no typing.** Holding the lens still on a word for about half
  a second is the whole gesture. A highlighter sweeps across the word as you
  hold, and when it finishes, the meaning slides up.
- **Instant.** Text is read on the phone itself, and the dictionary is inside
  the app (offline), so there's no network wait.
- **Easy language.** The most common meaning comes first, with an example and
  simpler synonyms. From v1.1, an optional AI explanation says what the word
  means *in this exact sentence*, in plain words.
- **Out of the way.** The meaning card never covers the lens, so you can move
  straight to the next word.

## What it is not

- Not a document scanner or a "photograph the page" app. There's no shutter,
  no saved photos, no cropping.
- Not a translation app (for now). It's about English words in English books.
